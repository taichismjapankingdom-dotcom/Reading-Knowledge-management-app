The history of Rome is vast and complex. Founded in 753 BC, it grew from a small town on the Tiber River into an empire that encompassed most of continental Europe, Britain, much of western Asia, northern Africa and the Mediterranean islands. 

During the Republic phase, power was distributed among the Senate and the consuls. However, class struggles between the patricians and plebeians frequently caused instability. Julius Caesar's crossing of the Rubicon marked the beginning of the end for the Republic. After his assassination in 44 BC, his adopted heir Octavian eventually rose to power, taking the title Augustus and becoming the first Roman Emperor.

The Empire brought a long period of peace and stability known as the Pax Romana. Trade flourished, and monumental architecture such as the Colosseum and the Pantheon were constructed. Engineering marvels like aqueducts and an extensive road network connected the vast territories.

However, over-expansion, economic troubles, and constant incursions by Germanic tribes eventually weakened the Western Roman Empire, leading to its traditional fall in 476 AD. The Eastern Roman Empire, or Byzantine Empire, continued for another thousand years until the fall of Constantinople in 1453.
